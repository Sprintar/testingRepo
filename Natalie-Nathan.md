
Weeknight, slow hour. Nathan is wiping down the bar when she takes a stool two seats from the corner—close to the exit, not hiding, not center stage.

“What can I get you?” he asks.

“Club soda. Lime.” No scan of the cocktail list, no hesitation.

He sets it down. “First time here.”

“Walked past a few times,” she says. “Looked quiet.”

Nathan nods. He watches the little things: short, clean nails; practical shoes; a watch worn on the inside of the wrist. She keeps her bag hooked on a knee instead of the back of the chair—city habits. “Name’s Nathan.”

“Natalie.”

They let the music fill a beat. He stacks coasters without looking; she notices anyway.

“So,” he says lightly, “you’re the Natalie who owed my sister a coffee.”

Her mouth tugs into a smile. “News travels.”

“She came home from Repair Saturday talking about a lamp that finally behaved and a person who asked good questions.” He shrugs. “I’m a brother. I listen when she’s excited. Then I try to—” he searches for the word “—read.”

“Read me, you mean.”

“Something like that.”

Natalie taps the rim of her glass. “What do you want to know?”

“What you do when you’re not making lamps feel seen.”

“Operations,” she says, then catches his raised eyebrow. “Real answer: I make sure projects don’t fall apart. Schedules, vendors, the unglamorous glue.”

“Useful,” he says. “My sister builds until the room disappears. Glue is… good.”

“And you?”

“Bartender, mostly. Part-time fixer of whatever Sophie forgot to label before it exploded.” He gestures at the neat labeler tucked under the counter. “I’m the zip-tie half of the team.”

“She told me,” Natalie says. “Said you’re the only person she trusts to hold the chassis when she’s soldering.”

He laughs. “That’s just because I stand still.”

He wipes a ring from the bar. “Why coffee?”

“Because she helped me and I wanted to say thanks in a way that wasn’t a rushed ‘you’re a lifesaver’ over a folding table,” Natalie says. “Also—” she pauses, choosing it “—I like how her face changes when she explains something she loves. It’s… bright.”

Nathan files that away. “She forgets to eat when she gets to bright.”

“I noticed,” Natalie says. “I brought a granola bar.”

“Points,” he says, deadpan.

They both smile. He lets a minute pass, the easy kind. Then: “Sophie’s had people treat her like a side quest. Nice to have around until something shinier pings their radar.”

“I’m not collecting achievements,” Natalie says. No edge, just steady. “If we hang out, it’s because I want to. If we don’t, I’ll say so.”

“Direct,” he says. “I like that.”

She tilts her head. “Your turn. You keep your exits clear, you stack things in even piles, and your playlist is timed to shoo the last five regulars out by eleven without them noticing.”

He snorts. “How’d I do?”

“Decent,” she says. “Protective, but you’re not trying to scare me off. Just… checking if I belong.”

“Guilty.”

He pulls a pen and writes a number on the back of a receipt. Slides it over. “If she texts you at midnight because something’s ‘almost compiling,’ remind her to drink water. If you can’t reach her, try me.”

Natalie tucks the slip into her wallet. “Deal.”

“And if you ever hurt her,” he adds, keeping the tone light, “I will overcharge you for club soda forever.”

“I’ll factor that into my life choices.”

They sit with the joke until it’s just air again. Natalie finishes her drink, sets the glass down in the dry circle he’d wiped for it.

“Thanks for the read,” she says, standing. “For what it’s worth, I don’t mind being a little unreadable at first. Makes conversations like this possible.”

“Fair,” he says. “Some mysteries can stay mysteries.”

She nods, pays, and starts for the door. Halfway, she glances back. “Tell Sophie the granola bar trick works better with peanut butter.”

“I’ll tell her,” he says.

When the door swings shut, Nathan looks at the empty glass, the neat lime wedge, the quiet corner she chose. He can’t pin her down, not completely. But he’s got enough: steady, careful, shows up with snacks. For his sister, that’s a good start.
